# Modes & Non-Diatonic Scales

## The Big Picture: Scales as Rotations and Transpositions

```
THE DIATONIC SET AND ITS MODES
C major scale: C D E F G A B  (7 notes, 5 whole + 2 half steps)

Rotate the starting point → 7 different modes, same pitch content:
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Start on   │ Mode   │ Pattern (W/H)        │ Character            │
├────────────┼────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤
│ C (I)      │ Ionian  │ W W H W W W H       │ Major = bright       │
│ D (II)     │ Dorian  │ W H W W W H W       │ Minor + raised 6th   │
│ E (III)    │ Phrygian│ H W W W H W W       │ Minor + ♭2, dark/Spanish│
│ F (IV)     │ Lydian  │ W W W H W W H       │ Major + ♯4, dreamy   │
│ G (V)      │ Mixolydian│ W W H W W H W     │ Major + ♭7, bluesy   │
│ A (VI)     │ Aeolian │ W H W W H W W       │ Natural minor        │
│ B (VII)    │ Locrian │ H W W H W W W       │ Minor + ♭2 + ♭5, unstable│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Key insight:** Modes are **not just transpositions** of a parent scale. D Dorian uses the same notes as C major but they function differently — D is tonic, D minor chord is home. The character comes from which intervals appear *relative to the tonic*.

## The Seven Church Modes in Detail

### Ionian (Major) — W W H W W W H

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  2  3  4  5  6  7
                       W  W  H  W  W  W  H
C Ionian: C D E F G A B C

Characteristic: P4 and P5 both present, M3 and M7 → bright, resolved
Use: Virtually all Western common-practice, pop, folk
     "Happy Birthday," most commercial music default
```

### Dorian — W H W W W H W

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  2  ♭3  4  5  6  ♭7
C Dorian: C D E♭ F G A B♭ C
(same notes as B♭ major)

Key feature: Minor mode (♭3) but RAISED 6th vs natural minor
  → not as dark as Aeolian
Use: Celtic folk (e.g., "Scarborough Fair"), jazz ("So What" – Miles Davis),
     funk (especially minor funk vamps), medieval modal, rock
     Guitar: "Oye Como Va" (Santana), "Eleanor Rigby" verse
```

### Phrygian — H W W W H W W

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  ♭2  ♭3  4  5  ♭6  ♭7
C Phrygian: C D♭ E♭ F G A♭ B♭ C
(same notes as A♭ major)

Key feature: ♭2 creates characteristic half-step above tonic → Spanish/Arabic sound
  The ♭II chord (D♭ major in C Phrygian) is heavily used in flamenco
Use: Flamenco, metal (heavy Phrygian riffs), Egyptian-flavored classical,
     game/film music for exotic/threatening contexts
     "White Rabbit" (Jefferson Airplane) — Phrygian feel
```

### Phrygian Dominant — H aug2 H W H W W

```
The 5th mode of harmonic minor:
C Phrygian dominant: C D♭ E♮ F G A♭ B♭ C
  (raised 3rd vs Phrygian — creates M3 + ♭2 combination)

The Jewish/Klezmer/Arabic flavor — the ♭2 + M3 combination
Use: Klezmer, Middle Eastern, Andalusian cadences, film "exotic" scoring
```

### Lydian — W W W H W W H

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  2  3  ♯4  5  6  7
C Lydian: C D E F♯ G A B C
(same notes as G major)

Key feature: ♯4 (augmented fourth from tonic) — the only "raised" major mode
  Creates a floating, unresolved, otherworldly quality
  The ♯4 avoids the P4's tendency to act as a dissonance over tonic
Use: John Williams (film music), Debussy, Bartók, Lydian b7 (jazz — see below)
     "Flying" cues in film scores almost always use Lydian
```

### Mixolydian — W W H W W H W

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  2  3  4  5  6  ♭7
C Mixolydian: C D E F G A B♭ C
(same notes as F major)

Key feature: Major mode (M3) but with ♭7 → no leading tone → open, unresolved feel
Use: Blues-based rock ("Sweet Home Chicago," "Norwegian Wood"),
     Celtic ("The Devil Went Down to Georgia"), most rock guitar solos,
     dominant chord substitution in jazz
     Every major-key rock song that ends on a ♭VII chord is Mixolydian
```

### Aeolian (Natural Minor) — W H W W H W W

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  2  ♭3  4  5  ♭6  ♭7
C Aeolian: C D E♭ F G A♭ B♭ C
(same notes as E♭ major — relative major is E♭)

The default minor mode. ♭3 ♭6 ♭7 vs major.
Use: Minor key music of all eras, folk, minor-key pop
     Differs from harmonic minor (raised 7th) and melodic minor (raised 6+7)
```

### Locrian — H W W H W W W

```
Intervals from tonic: 1  ♭2  ♭3  4  ♭5  ♭6  ♭7
C Locrian: C D♭ E♭ F G♭ A♭ B♭ C
(same notes as D♭ major)

Key feature: ♭5 — the tonic triad is DIMINISHED (C-E♭-G♭), maximally unstable
  No tonal center can be established — V chord would be a diminished triad (no P5)
Use: Almost never used as a tonal center. Appears as the vii° chord's scale.
     Metal uses "Locrian feel" without actually centering on it.
     Björk's "Army of Me" approaches Locrian
```

## Brightness Ranking

Modes can be ordered by brightness (more major-like notes = brighter):

```
BRIGHTEST                                              DARKEST
  Lydian > Ionian > Mixolydian > Dorian > Aeolian > Phrygian > Locrian
  (♯4)    (natural)  (♭7)       (♭3,♭7) (♭3,♭6,♭7) (♭2,♭3,♭6,♭7) (♭2,♭3,♭5,♭6,♭7)

Each step left = raise one note by a semitone
Each step right = lower one note by a semitone
```

This is a direct consequence of the Z₁₂ structure: each mode adds one more "flat" alteration from the major scale template.

## Pentatonic Scales

Remove two notes from the major scale to eliminate half-step tensions:

### Major Pentatonic

```
Remove scale degrees 4 and 7 (the half-step pair 3-4 and 7-8):
C major: C  D  E  F  G  A  B  C
                   ↑           ↑ remove these
C major pentatonic: C  D  E  G  A  (5 notes)
Pattern: W  W  m3  W  m3
Intervals from root: 1  2  3  5  6
```

### Minor Pentatonic

```
Remove scale degrees 2 and 6 from natural minor:
A natural minor: A  B  C  D  E  F  G  A
                    ↑           ↑ remove these
A minor pentatonic: A  C  D  E  G  (5 notes)
Pattern: m3  W  W  m3  W
Intervals from root: 1  ♭3  4  5  ♭7

Note: A minor pentatonic = C major pentatonic (same notes, different center)
```

### Blues Scale

Minor pentatonic + the "blue note" (♭5 / ♯4 = tritone from root):

```
A blues scale: A  C  D  D♯/E♭  E  G  A
                              ↑ added blue note

The E♭ creates half-step tension against both D and E
The ♭5 is "bent" in performance — not cleanly 6 semitones but approached as a slide
This note is NOT in any diatonic scale → the "outside" quality of blues
```

## Symmetric Scales

Scales built from repeating interval patterns — inherently equal-tempered:

### Whole-Tone Scale

```
Pattern: W W W W W W (6 notes, all whole steps)
C whole-tone: C  D  E  F♯  G♯  A♯  C

Properties:
  • Only 2 distinct whole-tone scales (transpositions 0 and 1 semitone)
  • Every interval is ambiguous — no perfect fifth, no leading tone
  • Every triad is augmented
  • Cannot establish tonal center
  • Used by Debussy for dreamy, unanchored quality
  • Dominant whole-tone scale = Lydian Dominant (1 2 3 ♯4 5 6 ♭7) often called "Lydian ♭7"
```

### Octatonic (Diminished) Scale

```
Two versions alternating H and W:
Diminished (H-W): H W H W H W H W (8 notes)
Dominant diminished (W-H): W H W H W H W H

C diminished (H-W): C  D♭  E♭  E  F♯  G  A  B♭  C
C dominant dim (W-H): C  D  E♭  F  F♯  A♭  A  B  C

Properties:
  • Only 3 distinct octatonic scales
  • Symmetry under transposition by minor thirds (3 semitones)
  • Every diminished 7th chord lives in the same octatonic set
  • Used for: jazz improvisation over dim7 / dom7♭9 chords,
    Stravinsky (Petrushka bitonal chord), Bartók, late Romantic tension
  • In jazz: play H-W over diminished chords, W-H over dominant chords
```

## Modal Jazz: Miles Davis and "Kind of Blue" (1959)

```
BEFORE MODAL JAZZ:              AFTER MODAL JAZZ:
Bebop chord changes             Static modal vamps
  ii–V–I every 2 bars             "So What" = D Dorian for 8 bars, E♭ Dorian for 4
  Rapid key changes               Improvise freely within the mode
  Chord-tone focused              Linear + intervallic exploration
  F major: Am7→D7→Gm7→C7→F       No "changes" to navigate
                                  Opens: melody, texture, space

Miles's solution: one scale per chord → simplify the harmony,
complexity comes from melodic invention within the mode.
```

Key tracks for modal analysis:
- **"So What"** — D Dorian vamp, binary form (D Dorian / E♭ Dorian / D Dorian)
- **"Flamenco Sketches"** — five scales in sequence, performer chooses duration on each
- **"Milestones"** (1958) — first modal piece, G Dorian vs G Aeolian contrast

## Modes in World Music and Pop

| Mode | Characteristic | Genre/Use | Signature sound |
|------|---------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Ionian | M3 + M7 | Pop, classical, everything | Bright/happy |
| Dorian | m3 + M6 | Jazz, Celtic, funk | "Minor with hope" |
| Phrygian | m2 | Flamenco, metal, Jewish | Dark, exotic, tense |
| Lydian | ♯4 | Film scoring, progressive | Floating, ethereal |
| Mixolydian | M3 + m7 | Blues rock, Celtic, folk | Major but earthy |
| Aeolian | m3 + m6 + m7 | Minor-key pop, classical | Default "sad" |
| Locrian | m2 + ♭5 | Metal, avant-garde | Unstable, angular |

**Pop examples:**
- Dorian: "Oye Como Va," "Evil Ways" (Santana), "Mad World" (Tears for Fears)
- Mixolydian: "Norwegian Wood," "Old Time Rock & Roll" (Seger), basically all blues rock
- Lydian: "Flying" (Beatles), John Williams everywhere, Danny Elfman
- Phrygian: "Wherever I May Roam" (Metallica), "War Pigs" riff (Sabbath)

## Advanced: Other Scales

### Harmonic Minor Modes

The harmonic minor scale generates its own 7 modes, including:

```
Mode 5 of harmonic minor = Phrygian Dominant
  (1 ♭2 3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7) — Spanish/Arabic sound

Mode 7 of harmonic minor = Altered Diminished (super-Locrian♯7)
  Used over diminished chords
```

### Jazz Melodic Minor Modes

Jazz melodic minor (ascending form only) generates key jazz scales:

```
Jazz melodic minor modes:
  Mode 1: C D E♭ F G A B  (jazz minor = "melodic minor")
  Mode 4: Lydian Dominant = 1 2 3 ♯4 5 6 ♭7  → over dominant chords
  Mode 6: Locrian ♯2       = 1 2 ♭3 4 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7 → over half-diminished
  Mode 7: Altered scale    = 1 ♭2 ♯2 3 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7 → over altered dominant (C7♯5♭9)
```

The altered scale (mode 7 of melodic minor) is the primary jazz scale for "outside" dominant resolution.

## Common Confusion Points

**"D Dorian" vs "transposing to D major":** D Dorian uses the same notes as C major but *centers on D*. Transposing to D major would give you D E F♯ G A B C♯ — completely different pitches. The mode is the context (what note is home), not a transposition of Ionian.

**The modes are not separate scales:** They're the same 7-note diatonic set heard through different ears. Every major scale contains all 7 modes simultaneously — which mode you're in depends entirely on which note functions as tonic.

**Pentatonic vs blues scale:** The blues scale is the minor pentatonic + one added chromatic note (the ♭5 / blue note). It's often treated as a 6-note "blues hexatonic." The blue note is not a stable resting point — it's used as a passing or bent note.

**Whole-tone: two scales only:** Because the 6 whole steps divide the octave exactly into 6 equal parts, transposing a whole-tone scale by 1 semitone gives you the other one. Transposing by 2 semitones gives you the same scale. There are literally only 2 distinct whole-tone collections.
