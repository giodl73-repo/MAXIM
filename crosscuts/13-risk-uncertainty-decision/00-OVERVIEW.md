# 13 — Risk, Uncertainty & Decision

## The Big Picture

This crosscut is the **People companion atlas**. It uses section number 13
because risk ultimately becomes human: a person or institution must act before
the world reveals the answer.

Methods of Knowing asks what warrant supports a claim. Risk, Uncertainty &
Decision asks the next question:

```
given incomplete knowledge, what action is justified?
```

```
RISK, UNCERTAINTY, AND DECISION

DECISION MAKER
  explorer, physician, engineer, commander, scientist, reformer, investor
      |
      v
SITUATION ---------> UNCERTAINTY ---------> STAKES
what is happening    what is unknown        what can be lost or gained
      |                    |                   |
      v                    v                   v
OPTIONS ----------> TRADEOFFS -----------> COMMITMENT
what can be done     what each option costs action closes alternatives
      |                    |                   |
      v                    v                   v
FEEDBACK ---------> REVISION -----------> RESPONSIBILITY
what happened        what must change      who owns the consequence

Risk is not danger alone.
Risk is consequence under uncertainty.
```

Read this as a **decision stack**. A decision maker faces a situation with
unknowns and stakes. Options create tradeoffs. Commitment closes some future
paths and opens others. Feedback updates the model. Revision changes the next
move. Responsibility remains even when uncertainty was real.

## Why This Belongs With People

Risk appears everywhere: finance, medicine, engineering, climate, software,
war, law, public health, design, agriculture, and exploration. But publishing it
with People makes the synthesis sharper.

```
uncertainty is abstract
decision is embodied
consequence is historical
```

| Person / Role | Risk Form | Decision Question |
|---|---|---|
| Explorer | navigation, weather, supplies | Continue, turn back, winter over, or split party? |
| Physician | diagnosis, treatment harm, prognosis | Treat now, test more, watch, or palliate? |
| Engineer | load, fatigue, failure, margin | Ship, redesign, inspect, derate, or shut down? |
| Scientist | evidence, reputation, inference | Publish, replicate, retract, or challenge? |
| Reformer | backlash, legitimacy, timing | Compromise, escalate, organize, or wait? |
| Commander | intelligence, logistics, casualties | Advance, hold, retreat, or negotiate? |
| Investor | volatility, liquidity, ruin | Allocate, hedge, exit, or double down? |
| Artist / architect | cost, reception, permanence | Conform, innovate, simplify, or risk failure? |

The human question is not "what is the optimal answer in hindsight?" It is:

```
what could a responsible actor know,
when did they know it,
what options remained,
and what did the stakes justify?
```

## Layer 1: Risk, Uncertainty, and Ignorance

These are not synonyms.

```
KNOWN OUTCOMES + KNOWN ODDS        -> risk
KNOWN OUTCOMES + UNKNOWN ODDS      -> uncertainty
UNKNOWN OUTCOMES                   -> ignorance
UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS                   -> surprise
```

| Condition | Example | Best First Move |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | bridge load distribution with known safety factors | quantify probability and consequence |
| Uncertainty | new disease severity in early outbreak | update fast, preserve options |
| Ignorance | first contact with unfamiliar ecosystem | probe cautiously, avoid irreversible moves |
| Ambiguity | conflicting intelligence or measurements | separate source reliability from interpretation |
| Volatility | market, weather, battlefield, wildfire | manage exposure and time horizon |
| Deep uncertainty | climate policy, AI governance, geopolitics | robust strategies over single forecast optimization |

The common mistake is treating uncertainty as if it were risk. When probabilities
are fake, a precise expected-value calculation creates false confidence.

## Layer 2: Stakes, Exposure, and Ruin

Risk is not just probability. A low-probability event with ruinous consequence
can dominate a decision.

```
expected loss = probability x consequence

but:
ruin changes the game
irreversibility changes the game
correlation changes the game
time pressure changes the game
```

| Risk Shape | Diagnostic Question | Typical Control |
|---|---|---|
| Frequent small loss | Can the system absorb repeated hits? | reserves, repair capacity, process improvement |
| Rare catastrophic loss | Can the system survive the tail? | redundancy, evacuation, hard limits |
| Irreversible loss | Can the choice be delayed or staged? | options, pilots, reversible experiments |
| Correlated loss | What fails together? | diversity, decoupling, geographic spread |
| Moral loss | Who bears harm if the decision is wrong? | consent, accountability, precaution |
| Reputational loss | What trust is consumed? | transparency, audit trail, admission path |

Ruin is special:

```
If one outcome ends the game,
average-case optimization is the wrong frame.
```

## Layer 3: Forecasts, Scenarios, and Options

A forecast predicts one path. A scenario explores several plausible paths. An
option preserves the ability to act later.

```
FORECAST
  "most likely path"

SCENARIOS
  "several coherent futures"

OPTIONS
  "rights without obligations"
```

| Tool | Good For | Dangerous When |
|---|---|---|
| Point forecast | stable, well-measured processes | used where regime change is plausible |
| Probability distribution | repeatable uncertainty | tails are thin because history is short |
| Scenario planning | strategic ambiguity | stories become entertainment without triggers |
| Real options | staged investment, exploration | option cost is ignored |
| Precautionary principle | irreversible severe harm | used to freeze all action regardless of tradeoff |
| Decision tree | explicit branching choices | probabilities are invented to fill boxes |

The best decision processes name **triggers**:

```
if evidence A appears -> escalate
if evidence B appears -> retreat
if cost C is crossed -> stop
if uncertainty D remains -> preserve option
```

## Layer 4: Decision Under Pressure

Some decisions allow analysis. Others allow only trained judgment.

```
TIME AVAILABLE

years        -> policy, infrastructure, research portfolio
months       -> product, campaign, construction sequence
days         -> outbreak response, expedition route, evacuation
minutes      -> surgery, flight, firefighting, incident response
seconds      -> reflex, drill, practiced protocol
```

| Pressure | What Helps | What Fails |
|---|---|---|
| Low time pressure | modeling, review, consultation, pilots | endless analysis |
| Moderate pressure | pre-mortems, red teams, staged commitments | consensus theater |
| High pressure | checklists, doctrine, drills, delegated authority | improvising governance |
| Extreme pressure | practiced pattern recognition | untrained heroics |

Expert judgment is not magic. It is compressed experience trained by feedback.
It works best in domains with repeated situations, timely feedback, and stable
rules. It degrades in domains with delayed feedback, politics, novelty, or rare
tail events.

---

## Layer 5: Accountability and Learning

A decision process is incomplete until it learns.

```
decision -> outcome -> evidence -> review -> doctrine -> next decision
```

| Review Mode | Best Use | Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Blameless postmortem | complex system incidents | becomes ritual without corrective action |
| Court / inquiry | public accountability | hindsight bias, legal defensiveness |
| Scientific replication | knowledge correction | publication incentives suppress null results |
| Medical morbidity review | clinical learning | individual blame hides system cause |
| Military after-action review | doctrine improvement | victory narrative suppresses failure data |
| Design critique | craft improvement | taste masquerades as analysis |

The hardest distinction:

```
bad outcome does not prove bad decision
good outcome does not prove good decision
```

Judge the decision by the information, options, stakes, and process available at
the time. Judge the learning by whether future decisions improve.

## What This Crosscut Is For

Use it when a situation has real stakes and incomplete information.

```
QUESTION                           FIRST DIAGNOSTIC MOVE

"Should we act now?"            -> compare delay risk with action risk
"How bad could this get?"       -> identify tail, ruin, and correlation
"Can we trust the forecast?"    -> inspect base rate, regime, and uncertainty
"What if we are wrong?"         -> preserve options and define stop triggers
"Who should decide?"            -> match authority to expertise and accountability
"Was that a bad decision?"      -> judge process, not outcome alone
```

The goal is not fear. The goal is **agency without delusion**: enough structure
to act, enough humility to revise.

## Decision Cheat Sheet

| If you need to diagnose... | Start With | Key Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Whether the situation is risk or uncertainty | Ask whether outcomes and probabilities are actually known | Fake probabilities are worse than acknowledged uncertainty |
| Whether a low-probability event matters | Check consequence, irreversibility, and ruin potential | Expected value can hide game-ending tail risk |
| Whether to wait or act | Compare the value of information with the cost of delay | More data is not free when the hazard is moving |
| Whether a forecast is useful | Inspect base rates, regime stability, calibration, and tail treatment | Forecasts degrade when the process changes |
| Whether scenario planning is serious | Look for decision triggers and option preservation | Scenarios without triggers become narrative decoration |
| Whether expert judgment is reliable | Check feedback quality, domain stability, and repetition | Experts fail when feedback is delayed or the domain has shifted |
| Whether accountability is fair | Reconstruct information, options, stakes, and time pressure at decision time | Hindsight bias turns outcome review into moral theater |
| Whether the system learned | Look for changed doctrine, controls, training, or incentives | A postmortem without changed behavior is archive, not learning |

## Common Confusion Points

**Risk is not fear** — Fear is an emotional signal. Risk is consequence under
uncertainty. Fear can reveal risk, exaggerate it, or point at the wrong object.

**Uncertainty is not ignorance** — Uncertainty may have known possibilities but
unknown probabilities. Ignorance means the possibility space itself is missing.
The right strategy changes when unknown unknowns are plausible.

**Precision is not confidence** — A spreadsheet can turn guessed inputs into
precise outputs. If probabilities are invented, the arithmetic is tidy but the
decision is not better warranted.

**Outcome quality is not decision quality** — Good decisions can lose and bad
decisions can win. The decision must be judged against what was knowable and
actionable at the time.

**Precaution is not paralysis** — Precaution is strongest for severe,
irreversible, poorly bounded harm. It still has to account for the harms of
inaction.
