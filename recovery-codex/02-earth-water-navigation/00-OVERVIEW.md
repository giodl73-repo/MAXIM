# Recovery 02 — Earth, Water & Navigation

## The Big Picture

This Recovery Codex volume belongs to **Earth & Space**. Its job is to preserve
the capability to read the planet: find water, understand terrain, navigate by
sky and map, anticipate weather, locate materials, and avoid rebuilding in
places that will destroy the work.

After measurement and transmission, recovery must become local. A future society
does not rebuild in abstraction. It rebuilds on a watershed, under a climate, on
soil, rock, floodplain, coast, desert, mountain, or island.

```
EARTH, WATER, AND NAVIGATION

Civilization starts by reading place.

SKY ---------> LAND ---------> WATER
sun, stars,    slope, soil,    source, flow,
weather        rock, route     storage, safety
   |              |               |
   v              v               v
MAP ---------> RESOURCE -----> SETTLEMENT
memory of       mineral, fuel,   where people
place           timber, clay     can persist
   |              |               |
   v              v               v
WARNING -----> ROUTE -------> STEWARDSHIP
flood, fire,    navigation,     keep the base
drought         trade, rescue   from collapse

Recovery is local before it is industrial.
Know where you are, what moves there, and what can fail.
```

Read this as a **place stack**. Sky gives direction, season, and warning. Land
sets routes, soils, hazards, and materials. Water determines survival. Maps
preserve spatial memory. Resources constrain making. Settlements concentrate
risk. Warnings save lives. Routes connect. Stewardship keeps the base usable.

---

## Minimum Viable Capability

A society has recovered this volume's core capability when it can:

1. Find, protect, move, store, and test water.
2. Read local terrain, drainage, soil, rock, vegetation, and hazard signs.
3. Navigate by sun, stars, landmarks, compass, map, and measured distance.
4. Make and update maps with scale, symbols, direction, date, and uncertainty.
5. Observe weather and seasonal patterns well enough to plan food, shelter, and
   travel.
6. Locate basic resources: clay, stone, timber, sand, lime, salt, ores, fuel, and
   arable soil.
7. Choose settlement sites that do not poison, flood, burn, starve, or isolate
   their inhabitants.

```
water -> terrain -> direction -> map -> weather -> resources -> settlement
```

The minimum is not global geoscience. The minimum is competent local earth
reading that can scale into surveying, hydrology, geology, meteorology, and
navigation.

| Level | Recovery Task | What Must Survive |
|---|---|---|
| Remember | Place determines what can be rebuilt | watershed, season, hazard, resource, route |

Recovery fails when a society remembers techniques but ignores place.

## Layer 1: Water First

Water is the first geography. It kills quickly when absent, slowly when
contaminated, and catastrophically when flood control is misunderstood.

```
source -> collection -> treatment -> storage -> distribution -> waste separation
```

| Water Task | Field Questions | Failure Signs |
|---|---|---|
| Find | Where does water collect, seep, flow, or condense? | dry channels mistaken for reliable supply |
| Protect | What contaminates upstream or downhill? | sickness clustered by source |
| Treat | Can sediment, microbes, chemicals, and salt be reduced? | clear water assumed safe |
| Store | Can containers stay covered, clean, shaded, and inspectable? | algae, insects, cracks, theft |
| Move | Can gravity, pumps, channels, or animals move it safely? | leaks, erosion, stagnant zones |
| Separate waste | Where do latrines, animals, drains, and runoff go? | wells downhill from waste |

Water rule:

```
safe source + safe path + safe storage + waste separation
```

Miss any one, and disease will teach the lesson.

## Layer 2: Terrain, Soil, and Drainage

Terrain is infrastructure before humans build anything.

```
slope + soil + vegetation + water path -> usable ground
```

| Ground Sign | What It Suggests | Recovery Use |
|---|---|---|
| Valley bottom | water access, fertile sediment | farming, route, flood risk |
| Terrace | old river level, better drainage | safer settlement than floodplain |
| Ridge | visibility, dry ground | route, defense, wind exposure |
| Wetland plants | high water table | water source, disease and footing risk |
| Cracked clay | shrink-swell soil | foundation caution, pottery resource |
| Gravel bar | high-energy stream | aggregate, flood warning |
| Landslide scar | unstable slope | avoid settlement, watch roads |
| Salt crust | evaporation, salinity | crop and drinking-water caution |

Settlement diagnostic:

```
where does water go in the worst hour, worst season, and worst decade?
```

If nobody can answer, do not build permanently there.

## Layer 3: Navigation Without Fragile Systems

Navigation begins with repeatable direction and distance, not satellites.

```
direction + distance + landmark + record -> route
```

| Method | Use | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Sun path | east-west, time of day, season | varies with latitude and season |
| Stars | night direction and latitude clues | clouds, horizon, learning curve |
| Shadow stick | noon line, rough latitude practice | needs repeated observation |
| Compass | magnetic direction | declination, local iron, damage |
| Pace / rope / wheel | distance estimate | terrain and fatigue distort |
| Landmark chaining | practical travel route | fails in fog, snow, darkness, changed landscape |
| Dead reckoning | route when landmarks absent | error accumulates |
| Map and bearing | route planning | map age and scale matter |

Navigation rule:

```
never trust one cue alone
combine sky, ground, instrument, distance, and local testimony
```

## Layer 4: Maps as Spatial Memory

A map is not the territory. It is a record of spatial claims.

```
observation -> measurement -> symbol -> scale -> map -> route decision
```

| Map Element | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| North / orientation | prevents rotation errors |
| Scale | turns drawing into distance |
| Date | warns of changed rivers, roads, settlements, coastlines |
| Legend | makes symbols readable |
| Contours / slope marks | reveal water, route, and hazard |
| Water sources | survival and settlement planning |
| Hazards | flood, fire, slide, disease, conflict |
| Resources | clay, timber, stone, ore, fuel, arable land |
| Uncertainty marks | prevent false confidence |

Mapmaking practice:

```
sketch -> measure -> compare -> walk the route -> revise -> copy with date
```

An old map is evidence, not command. Treat it as a claim requiring field check.

## Layer 5: Weather, Season, and Sky

Weather is not just inconvenience. It determines food, travel, shelter, disease,
fire, water, and morale.

| Observation | Recovery Use | Failure If Ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Wind direction | storms, smoke, sailing, fire risk | bad shelter and fire decisions |
| Clouds | short-term weather signs | exposed travel and harvest loss |
| Pressure trend | storm warning | surprise weather shifts |
| Rainfall log | water and crop planning | overbuilding on lucky years |
| Frost dates | planting and storage | crop loss |
| Heat and humidity | labor, disease, food spoilage | exhaustion, mold, infection |
| Snowpack | spring water forecast | flood or drought surprise |
| Drought signs | rationing and migration planning | late collapse |

Weather recovery ladder:

```
look daily -> record daily -> compare seasons -> identify extremes -> plan buffers
```

Do not design for average weather. Design for the event that breaks the system.

## Layer 6: Resources and Geological Clues

Earth supplies the first materials for recovery.

| Resource | Field Clue | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Clay | sticky fine soil, cracking, river deposits | pottery, brick, seal, kiln work |
| Sand / gravel | bars, terraces, outwash | mortar, concrete aggregate, filtration |
| Limestone | fizz with acid, fossils, pale rock | lime, cement, soil amendment |
| Timber | forest type, straight grain, access | shelter, tools, fuel, charcoal |
| Salt | evaporite crust, brine, salt plants, coast | food preservation, health, trade |
| Iron ore | red/brown heavy rock, bog iron | tools, fasteners, machines |
| Copper minerals | green/blue staining | wiring, alloys, vessels |
| Coal / peat | black layers, bog deposits | heat, fuel, industrial transition |
| Obsidian / flint | conchoidal fracture | cutting tools |

Resource rule:

```
find -> test -> map -> protect -> extract slowly enough to learn
```

Bad extraction can poison water, destroy soil, and erase the resource base.

## Cargo-Cult Traps

| Trap | Why It Fails | Recovery Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Clear water assumed safe | microbes and chemicals may be invisible | source protection, treatment, testing, disease logs |
| Settlement by convenience | easy ground may flood, burn, or poison | inspect worst season and historical signs |
| Map treated as territory | maps age and simplify | field verification and revision dates |
| Average weather planning | extremes break systems | record range, not just mean |
| One-cue navigation | compass, stars, or landmarks can mislead | combine independent cues |
| Resource rush | extraction damages water and soil | test, map, pace, and monitor |
| Imported farming calendar | seasons differ locally | local phenology and rainfall records |
| Ignored downstream effects | waste and erosion move | watershed thinking |

## Practical Reconstruction Sequence

```
1. identify safest immediate water sources
2. separate waste from water and food
3. map settlement, water, hazards, routes, and resources
4. establish direction, distance, and local survey baselines
5. start daily weather and water logs
6. test soils and choose fields by drainage and season
7. locate clay, sand, timber, stone, lime, salt, and fuel
8. build protected storage: water, seed, maps, records
9. train navigators, mapmakers, weather observers, and water stewards
10. revise maps after flood, fire, drought, conflict, and migration
```

Each field record should include:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Date and observer | trust and seasonal comparison |
| Location and route | repeatability |
| Weather | context |
| Measurement method | comparability |
| Sketch or symbol | spatial memory |
| Uncertainty | prevents overconfidence |
| Follow-up needed | creates continuity |

## Decision Cheat Sheet

| If you need to... | Start With | Key Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Find a settlement site | Water, drainage, flood history, soil, wind, fuel, route, and waste separation | Convenient sites often hide seasonal hazards |
| Make water safer | Protect source, separate waste, settle/filter, boil/disinfect, store covered | Clear water can still kill |
| Navigate without satellites | Combine sun, stars, compass, landmarks, distance, and local records | Every single cue has failure modes |
| Make a usable map | Include orientation, scale, date, legend, water, hazards, resources, and uncertainty | A map is a dated claim |
| Plan agriculture | Observe soil, slope, frost, rainfall, water access, pests, and storage climate | Imported calendars can fail locally |
| Locate materials | Read landforms, test samples, map deposits, and monitor extraction effects | Resource recovery can destroy the water base |
| Prepare for weather | Keep daily logs and design for extremes | Average conditions do not test resilience |
| Teach earth reading | Use field walks, sketch maps, repeated observations, and local elders' knowledge | Place knowledge dies when it is not practiced outdoors |

## Common Confusion Points

**Water access is not water safety** — A source can be abundant and dangerous.
Waste separation and source protection matter as much as finding water.

**Maps are arguments** — They choose scale, symbols, date, and omissions. Trust
them as evidence, not as reality itself.

**Navigation is redundancy** — Good navigation combines independent cues because
clouds, magnets, fatigue, memory, and landmarks can all fail.

**Weather is infrastructure** — A settlement that ignores wind, rainfall, frost,
heat, and extremes has already chosen its failure mode.

**Resources are part of watersheds** — Mining, quarrying, timber cutting, and
fuel gathering can destroy the very land and water recovery depends on.
