# Recovery 04 — Health, Body & Disease

## The Big Picture

This Recovery Codex volume belongs to **Life Sciences**. Its job is to preserve
the capability to keep bodies alive, reduce suffering, prevent disease, manage
birth and injury, and rebuild medicine without turning fragments of modern
health care into ritual.

Health recovery begins before doctors. It begins with clean water, sanitation,
nutrition, shelter, sleep, warmth, injury prevention, childbirth safety, and
infection control. Advanced medicine grows from that base; it does not replace
it.

```
HEALTH, BODY, AND DISEASE

Medicine starts with prevention and observation.

WATER -------> SANITATION ---> NUTRITION
safe source     waste away      calories, protein,
and storage     from bodies     fat, micronutrients
   |               |               |
   v               v               v
BODY --------> DISEASE -----> CARE
signs, pulse,    infection,      rest, fluids,
breath, pain     injury, birth   warmth, skill
   |               |               |
   v               v               v
RECORD ------> TREATMENT ---> PUBLIC HEALTH
case, dose,      procedure,      quarantine,
outcome          medicine        vaccination,
                                 surveillance

The first hospital is clean water plus trained hands.
The first drug is prevention that makes treatment unnecessary.
```

Read this as a **care stack**. Water, sanitation, and nutrition prevent much
disease. Body signs guide triage. Disease knowledge separates infection, injury,
deficiency, poisoning, chronic illness, pregnancy, and mental distress. Care
stabilizes. Records teach. Treatments must be tested. Public health protects
whole populations.

---

## Minimum Viable Capability

A society has recovered this volume's core capability when it can:

1. Provide safe water, sanitation, nutrition, shelter, and warmth.
2. Recognize danger signs: bleeding, breathing trouble, dehydration, fever,
   altered consciousness, childbirth emergency, infection, poisoning, shock.
3. Prevent infection with handwashing, clean instruments, wound care, isolation,
   waste separation, and food safety.
4. Manage common injuries, burns, diarrhea, fever, respiratory illness, birth,
   pain, and chronic vulnerability with known limits.
5. Keep case records, dose records, outcomes, and outbreak logs.
6. Train caregivers, midwives, sanitation stewards, and public-health observers.
7. Distinguish evidence-based treatment from tradition, rumor, and desperation.

```
prevent -> recognize -> stabilize -> record -> treat -> teach -> protect population
```

The minimum is not recreating a modern hospital. It is rebuilding the chain that
keeps preventable death from becoming normal.

## The Recovery Ladder

| Level | Recovery Task | What Must Survive |
|---|---|---|
| Remember | Prevention saves more lives than heroic treatment | water, sanitation, nutrition, vaccination memory, quarantine logic |
| Recognize | Identify body signs, disease patterns, injury severity, and emergencies | symptom guides, anatomy basics, triage cues |
| Measure | Track temperature, pulse, breathing, weight, dose, hydration, outbreak counts | thermometers, clocks, scales, records |
| Rebuild | Establish clean care spaces, birth practice, wound care, pharmacies, surveillance | soap, clean cloth, boiling, instruments, logs |
| Teach | Train caregivers, midwives, public-health workers, and household first responders | drills, case review, apprenticeships, ethics |

Recovery fails when medical artifacts survive but the prevention, sanitation,
measurement, and care system around them disappears.

## Layer 1: Sanitation, Water, and Food Safety

Most medical recovery is upstream of the clinic.

```
feces / waste / vectors / spoiled food -> bodies -> disease
```

| Prevention Task | Why It Matters | Failure Sign |
|---|---|---|
| Separate feces from water | prevents diarrheal disease | clustered diarrhea, child deaths |
| Wash hands and tools | breaks transmission | wound infections, birth fever |
| Boil or disinfect unsafe water | reduces microbes | recurring waterborne illness |
| Cook and store food safely | prevents poisoning and parasites | vomiting clusters, mold, pests |
| Control insects and rodents | reduces vector and storage disease | bites, fever cycles, grain loss |
| Ventilate smoke and crowding | protects lungs | cough clusters, eye irritation |
| Manage corpses safely | reduces exposure and panic | unsafe rituals, contamination |

Sanitation rule:

```
clean water in, waste out, hands clean, food protected, air breathable
```

If this layer fails, treatment capacity will be overwhelmed.

## Layer 2: Body Signs and Triage

Triage is disciplined attention under scarcity.

```
look -> listen -> touch -> measure -> decide
```

| Sign | Why It Matters | Urgent Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Airway / breathing | oxygen is immediate | obstruction, pneumonia, asthma, drowning |
| Bleeding | circulation fails quickly | hemorrhage, shock |
| Consciousness | brain perfusion and injury | head injury, infection, poisoning |
| Fever | infection or inflammation | sepsis, malaria-like illness, dehydration |
| Pulse | circulation and stress | shock, arrhythmia, fever burden |
| Hydration | diarrhea and heat kill fast | sunken eyes, no urine, lethargy |
| Pain location | injury or internal disease | abdomen, chest, head, childbirth |
| Skin signs | perfusion, rash, infection | blue, pale, hot, spreading redness |

Triage rule:

```
airway, breathing, bleeding, consciousness, hydration, infection spread
```

Treat the immediate killer first, even when the deeper cause is unknown.

## Layer 3: Infection Control and Wound Care

Infection control is a technology of boundaries.

```
microbe source -> route -> susceptible body
```

| Route | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Dirty hands | handwashing, clean workflow |
| Dirty instruments | boiling, heat, chemical disinfection where known safe |
| Open wound | cleaning, debridement skill, clean dressing, monitoring |
| Droplet / air | distance, ventilation, masks when useful, isolation |
| Water / food | treatment, cooking, protected storage |
| Vector | nets, drainage, habitat control, protective clothing |
| Birth canal / instruments | clean birth surface, clean hands, clean cord care |

Wound-care basics:

```
stop bleeding -> clean -> remove dead material if trained -> cover -> monitor
```

Red flags: spreading redness, pus, fever, foul smell, increasing pain, black
tissue, red streaks, confusion, shock.

## Layer 4: Medicines, Procedures, and Evidence

Medicines are powerful because dose, timing, purity, indication, and
contraindication matter.

| Treatment Type | Recovery Requirement | Cargo-Cult Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Oral rehydration | correct salt/sugar/water ratio | too concentrated or too dilute |
| Pain control | dose, toxicity, dependency risk | masking emergency signs |
| Antibiotics | correct indication, dose, duration | resistance, allergy, false confidence |
| Herbal medicines | species, part, preparation, dose | lookalike, variable potency |
| Surgery / procedures | anatomy, cleanliness, indication, aftercare | heroic harm |
| Vaccination | cold chain, schedule, public trust | ritual without viable material |
| Chronic medicines | continuity and monitoring | sudden withdrawal, wrong dose |

Treatment record:

```
patient -> problem -> treatment -> dose/procedure -> response -> adverse effect -> follow-up
```

If outcome records are not kept, medicine cannot learn.

## Layer 5: Birth, Children, and Vulnerability

Recovery societies are judged by whether mothers, infants, children, elders, and
disabled people survive ordinary hazards.

| Care Domain | Essential Capability | Danger Sign |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy | nutrition, clean birth planning, danger recognition | bleeding, seizures, fever, obstructed labor |
| Birth | clean hands, clean surface, clean cord, warmth | prolonged labor, heavy bleeding, newborn not breathing |
| Newborn | warmth, feeding, infection monitoring | cold, poor feeding, fever, lethargy |
| Children | hydration, nutrition, vaccination memory, growth tracking | diarrhea, wasting, respiratory distress |
| Elders | mobility, hydration, medication continuity, falls prevention | confusion, dehydration, isolation |
| Disability | access, assistive tools, social protection | neglect, exclusion |

Birth rule:

```
clean, warm, watched, and ready to escalate
```

Traditional knowledge matters, but it must be paired with outcome review.

## Layer 6: Public Health and Outbreak Control

Public health is medicine at population scale.

```
case -> pattern -> source -> intervention -> monitor
```

| Public-Health Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| Case definition | decides what counts |
| Outbreak log | detects clusters |
| Isolation / quarantine | interrupts transmission |
| Contact tracing | finds spread paths |
| Vaccination records | protects population memory |
| Sanitation inspection | prevents recurrence |
| Food and water testing | locates sources |
| Risk communication | keeps trust under fear |

Outbreak rule:

```
define cases, count cases, map cases, separate routes, communicate honestly
```

Panic and denial both spread disease.

## Cargo-Cult Traps

| Trap | Why It Fails | Recovery Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Heroic treatment over prevention | clinics drown in preventable illness | prioritize water, sanitation, nutrition |
| Drug-name preservation | dose, purity, indication, and supply vanish | preserve pharmacology, records, contraindications |
| Sterile-looking tools | appearance is not cleanliness | heat, disinfection, workflow, logs |
| Tradition without outcome review | harmful practices persist | record results and revise |
| Quarantine without trust | people hide illness | transparent rules, support, appeal |
| One healer bottleneck | knowledge dies or becomes power | train many caregivers |
| Symptom suppression | danger signs hidden | treat cause and monitor |
| Ignoring mental distress | trauma breaks recovery capacity | care, community, safety, meaning |

## Practical Reconstruction Sequence

```
1. protect water and separate waste
2. establish handwashing, soap, clean cooking, and food storage
3. train household first response: bleeding, dehydration, fever, breathing
4. create clean care spaces and clean birth kits
5. start case records, dose records, and outbreak logs
6. build wound-care, oral rehydration, fever-care, and nutrition protocols
7. identify and verify local medicines with dose and toxicity records
8. train midwives, caregivers, sanitation stewards, and public-health observers
9. create isolation and risk-communication procedures
10. review outcomes and revise practice openly
```

Each case record should include:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Person and age | risk and follow-up |
| Date and place | outbreak pattern |
| Symptoms and signs | diagnosis and triage |
| Exposure history | source finding |
| Treatment and dose | safety and learning |
| Outcome | evidence |
| Caregiver | accountability |

## Decision Cheat Sheet

| If you need to... | Start With | Key Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce deaths fastest | Safe water, sanitation, nutrition, warmth, and clean hands | Prevention beats heroic treatment |
| Triage illness or injury | Airway, breathing, bleeding, consciousness, hydration, infection spread | Treat immediate killers first |
| Prevent infection | Clean hands, clean tools, protected wounds, safe food/water, isolation when needed | Clean-looking is not sterile |
| Use medicine safely | Confirm indication, dose, purity, contraindication, duration, and follow-up | Drug names without practice are dangerous |
| Manage childbirth | Clean birth, warmth, observation, danger signs, escalation plan | Normal birth can become emergency quickly |
| Detect outbreak | Define cases, count, map, trace, communicate, and intervene | Fear hides cases if trust is absent |
| Teach care | Use drills, case review, apprenticeships, and outcome records | Compassion without skill is insufficient |
| Preserve medical knowledge | Keep anatomy, physiology, sanitation, pharmacology, case records, and public-health logic | Medicine without records cannot improve |

## Common Confusion Points

**Medicine is not only treatment** — Most lives are saved by prevention,
sanitation, nutrition, vaccination, shelter, and public health.

**Clean is a process, not an appearance** — Instruments, hands, water, and
surfaces require known cleaning methods and workflows.

**Dose makes the medicine** — Too little may fail. Too much may poison. The same
substance can heal or harm.

**Isolation is social technology** — It works only with trust, support, clear
rules, and honest communication.

**Traditional and modern are not quality categories** — The question is evidence,
dose, safety, mechanism, outcome, and context.
