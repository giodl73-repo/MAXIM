# Recovery 01 — Living World & Food Webs

## The Big Picture

This Recovery Codex volume belongs to **Natural World**. Its job is to preserve
the capability to recognize, protect, cultivate, harvest, and live within the
living systems that keep people fed and landscapes functional.

Recovery does not start with agriculture alone. It starts with soil organisms,
plants, animals, fungi, pollinators, pests, water, seed, season, waste, and
food webs. A society that can grow crops while destroying soil is not recovering;
it is spending biological capital.

```
LIVING WORLD AND FOOD WEBS

Food is ecology made edible.

SOIL --------> PLANT -------> ANIMAL
microbes,       crop, tree,    herd, flock,
fungi, humus    wild food      fish, insect
   |              |              |
   v              v              v
SEED --------> SEASON -----> HARVEST
memory of       timing,        collection,
adaptation      weather        storage
   |              |              |
   v              v              v
WASTE -------> WEB --------> STEWARDSHIP
compost,        pollinator,    keep the
manure          pest, predator base alive

The living base is not a resource pile.
It is a reproducing system with limits.
```

Read this as a **food-web stack**. Soil makes fertility. Plants turn light, water,
and minerals into food. Animals move nutrients and risk. Seeds carry adaptation.
Season controls timing. Harvest must be stored. Waste must return safely.
Food webs regulate pests and pollination. Stewardship keeps the system
reproductive.

---

## Minimum Viable Capability

A society has recovered this volume's core capability when it can:

1. Identify safe staple foods, dangerous lookalikes, medicinal plants, pests,
   pollinators, and useful animals.
2. Maintain soil fertility through compost, manure, cover, rotation, and erosion
   control.
3. Save, select, store, and exchange seeds without losing local adaptation.
4. Grow calories, protein, fats, fiber, fuel, and medicinal plants across seasons.
5. Domesticate, herd, hunt, fish, or manage animals without collapsing breeding
   populations.
6. Store food safely against moisture, insects, rodents, mold, and theft.
7. Preserve biodiversity enough that disease, drought, pests, and climate shocks
   do not erase the food base.

```
identify -> cultivate -> save seed -> manage animals -> store -> restore fertility
```

The minimum is not maximum yield. The minimum is a food system that can reproduce
itself.

| Level | Recovery Task | What Must Survive |
|---|---|---|

Recovery fails when food is treated as a product instead of a living system.

## Layer 1: Soil Is the First Farm

Soil is not dirt. It is mineral structure plus water, air, organic matter,
microbes, fungi, roots, animals, and time.

```
rock particles + organic matter + water + air + life -> fertile soil
```

| Soil Sign | What It Suggests | Recovery Action |
|---|---|---|
| Dark crumb structure | organic matter and aggregation | protect, mulch, avoid compaction |
| Pale sand | drainage, low nutrient holding | add compost, protect moisture |
| Heavy clay | nutrient holding, drainage risk | avoid wet compaction, add organic matter |
| Earthworms / roots | biological activity | preserve cover and moisture |
| Crust / gullies | erosion and raindrop impact | cover soil, slow runoff |
| Sour smell / waterlogging | low oxygen | improve drainage and raised beds |
| Salt crust | salinity and evaporation | drainage, freshwater leaching if possible |

Soil rule:

```
keep it covered, fed, rooted, drained, and alive
```

Bare soil is hunger arriving later.

## Layer 2: Plants, Seeds, and Local Adaptation

Seeds are biological memory. They remember climate, soil, disease pressure,
human selection, and taste.

```
plant -> seed -> selection -> storage -> next season
```

| Seed Task | Why It Matters | Failure Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Identify variety | preserves traits | mixed or mislabeled seed |
| Select parents | improves local fit | saving from weak plants |
| Dry properly | prevents mold and rot | lost germination |
| Store cool and dry | preserves viability | insects, humidity, heat |
| Test germination | prevents false inventory | planting dead seed |
| Avoid unwanted crossing | keeps traits stable when needed | variety drift |
| Exchange carefully | increases diversity | imports pests or maladapted seed |

Crop recovery needs diversity:

```
staple calories + legumes + oil/fat + greens + roots + fruit + fiber + medicine
```

Do not build a food system around one miracle crop.

## Layer 3: Animals, Fish, Insects, and Useful Wildness

Animals convert landscapes humans cannot eat into food, labor, fiber, manure,
transport, and ecological services. They also carry disease, overgraze, and
collapse if breeding is mismanaged.

| Living Partner | Recovery Use | Stewardship Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Chickens / ducks | eggs, meat, pest control, manure | predators, disease, feed competition |
| Goats / sheep | milk, meat, fiber, brush control | overgrazing |
| Cattle / buffalo | traction, milk, meat, manure | water, pasture, injury |
| Bees / pollinators | pollination, honey, wax | pesticide, habitat loss, disease |
| Fish | protein, nutrient cycling | overharvest, water quality |
| Dogs | guarding, hunting, herding | disease, uncontrolled breeding |
| Wild game | emergency protein | population collapse |
| Insects | pollination, food, decomposition | pest outbreak if webs break |

Animal rule:

```
breeding stock is capital, not emergency meat
```

Eat the breeding base and the future disappears.

## Layer 4: Food Webs, Pests, and Disease

Pest control is ecological management before it is poison.

```
crop -> pest -> predator -> habitat -> balance
```

| Pattern | Meaning | Recovery Response |
|---|---|---|
| Sudden pest outbreak | monoculture, weak plants, predator loss, weather shift | diversify, inspect soil, protect predators |
| Pollination failure | pollinator loss, weather, timing mismatch | plant habitat, diversify bloom times |
| Livestock disease | crowding, sanitation, new pathogen | quarantine, clean water, breeding records |
| Crop disease | humidity, seed infection, soil carryover | rotation, resistant seed, spacing |
| Rodent storage loss | access and waste | sealed storage, cats/dogs/traps, sanitation |
| Algal or fish die-off | nutrient overload, low oxygen, heat | reduce runoff, shade, aeration if possible |

Food-web diagnostic:

```
what eats it, what feeds it, what pollinates it, what decomposes it?
```

If you cannot answer, you are farming blind.

## Layer 5: Harvest, Storage, and Nutrition

Growing food is not enough. Food must remain edible and nutritionally adequate
through the hungry season.

| Storage Method | Use | Failure Signs |
|---|---|---|
| Drying | grains, beans, fruit, herbs, meat | mold, insects, incomplete drying |
| Salting / brining | meat, fish, vegetables | wrong salt level, contamination |
| Fermentation | vegetables, dairy, grains | off smells, unsafe vessels, wrong temperature |
| Smoking | meat, fish | surface preserved but interior unsafe |
| Root cellar | roots, apples, cabbage | rot, rodents, freezing |
| Sealed bins | grain, seed | condensation, weevils, rodents |
| Oil / fat storage | calories, cooking, lamps | rancidity |

Nutrition rule:

```
calories + protein + fat + micronutrients + clean water
```

Starvation can occur with a full belly if the diet is too narrow.

## Cargo-Cult Traps

| Trap | Why It Fails | Recovery Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum yield worship | mines soil and increases fragility | track soil, diversity, storage, and resilience |
| One-crop dependence | pests, disease, or weather can erase food | polyculture, rotations, seed diversity |
| Seed without selection | local adaptation degrades | select, label, test, exchange carefully |
| Clear land everywhere | destroys pollinators, predators, water regulation | keep habitat patches and corridors |
| Eat breeding stock | solves today by starving tomorrow | protect breeding base |
| Store damp food | creates mold and toxins | dry, inspect, ventilate, rotate |
| Poison-first pest control | kills helpers and creates rebound | ecological diagnosis first |
| Ignore wild knowledge | loses emergency foods and indicators | maintain field guides and local teachers |

## Practical Reconstruction Sequence

```
1. identify safe water, edible staples, and poisonous lookalikes
2. protect soil from erosion and compaction
3. start gardens with diverse quick crops and staples
4. establish compost, manure handling, and waste safety
5. save and label seed from healthy local plants
6. build dry, pest-resistant food and seed storage
7. inventory calories, protein, fat, and micronutrients
8. protect pollinator and predator habitat
9. manage breeding animals as long-term capital
10. teach seasonal calendars, field identification, and food preservation
```

Each food record should include:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Species / variety | identification and selection |
| Source | provenance and adaptation |
| Planting / harvest date | seasonal learning |
| Yield and storage loss | food planning |
| Weather and soil notes | interpretation |
| Pest / disease signs | prevention |
| Seed saved | continuity |

## Decision Cheat Sheet

| If you need to... | Start With | Key Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Rebuild food quickly | Combine safe foraging, gardens, staple crops, and storage | Emergency food is not a stable food system |
| Protect fertility | Keep soil covered, fed, rooted, drained, and biologically active | Soil loss is harder to recover than crop loss |
| Save seed | Select healthy local plants, dry, label, store, and test germination | Seed is adaptation memory |
| Manage animals | Protect breeding stock, water, feed, sanitation, and predator control | Animals can amplify both resilience and disease |
| Control pests | Inspect soil, diversity, predators, crop spacing, and storage hygiene | Poison-first thinking can collapse helpers |
| Prevent famine | Track inventories, diversity, storage losses, and next planting seed | Eating seed grain converts shortage into disaster |
| Use wild foods | Require strong identification and local confirmation | Lookalikes can kill |
| Teach food recovery | Use field walks, seasonal calendars, seed logs, cooking, and preservation practice | Food knowledge must be embodied and local |

## Common Confusion Points

**Agriculture is not just planting** — It includes soil, water, seed, pests,
storage, labor, animals, weather, and institutions.

**Wild does not mean safe** — Wild foods and medicines require identification,
dose knowledge, season, preparation, and local confirmation.

**Seed is not generic** — Seed carries local adaptation. Losing it can set a
region back years.

**Pests are signals** — Outbreaks often point to soil stress, monoculture,
storage weakness, predator loss, or weather shifts.

**Food security is not only calories** — Protein, fat, micronutrients, clean
water, and storage stability all matter.
